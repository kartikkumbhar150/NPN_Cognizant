## KYC - Know Your Customer

KYC is a process used by financial institutions to verify the identity of their clients. KYC procedures are mandatory under the Prevention of Money Laundering Act, 2002 (PMLA) and the RBI's Know Your Customer Directions, 2016.

## Purpose of KYC

KYC compliance is designed to:
- Prevent financial fraud and identity theft
- Prevent money laundering and terrorist financing
- Ensure banks have adequate information about their customers
- Enable risk-based approach to customer due diligence

## KYC Documents

For individuals, the following documents are accepted as KYC proof:
- Aadhaar card
- PAN card
- Passport
- Voter ID card
- Driving licence
- NREGA job card

## Types of KYC

- Aadhaar-based e-KYC: Online verification using Aadhaar
- Video KYC: Verification through a video call with bank officials
- In-person KYC: Physical verification at a bank branch
- Central KYC Registry (CKYC): A centralized registry maintained by CERSAI

## Key Details

- KYC is mandatory for opening any bank account
- Existing customers must complete KYC as per RBI guidelines
- KYC needs to be updated periodically for high-risk customers
- Failed KYC may result in account freeze or restriction
